*To the one who remembers covenant*

# Read as a witness.

Jewish tradition knows the discipline of remembering: creation remembered in rest, liberation remembered in ritual, covenant remembered through word and deed. Memory is not nostalgia. It is a way of making the past morally present.

This Quranic verse speaks of signs that will become visible across the horizons and within the self. Its closing image is witness. Read from within a tradition shaped by testimony, the question is immediate: what has the world been saying, and what does the hearer owe to what has been heard?

---

> سنريهم اياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق أولم يكف بربك أنه على كل شيء شهيد
>
> *We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness?*

## A readable world

The horizon carries the marks of order, limit, and dependence. Day follows night; life is received rather than self-created; human power meets boundaries it did not choose. To call these things signs is to refuse a sealed universe. The ordinary can bear meaning without ceasing to be ordinary.

Signs also unfold through time. History exposes pride, preserves unexpected communities, and turns private choices into public consequences. A sign may be seen in a moment, or only after generations have taught one another how to notice it.

## The divided heart

The verse then enters the self. Here too there is testimony: conscience, longing, resistance, gratitude, and the persistent human ability to rationalize what we already know is wrong. The inner world is not automatically pure, but it is revealing.

Self-examination matters because covenantal life is lived through action. Truth is not only contemplated; it is answered. A sign becomes ethically serious when it changes how the vulnerable are treated, how power is restrained, and how faithfully one speaks.

## Before the Witness

Islam and Judaism make distinct claims, and respectful reading should not disguise them. Yet both resist the reduction of God to an image or an object within creation. The verse ends by locating ultimate testimony with the Lord who sees all things.

That ending makes humility possible. No reader possesses the whole horizon, and no person sees even themselves without distortion. We read, remember, argue, and act before a Witness whose knowledge exceeds our own.
