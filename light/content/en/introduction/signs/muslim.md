*To the one who already believes*

# Look again.

Faith does not make the signs ordinary. Familiarity can soften wonder, and a verse repeated many times can pass the lips before it reaches the heart. This invitation asks the believer to return to the world with attention: to see creation not as scenery, but as testimony.

The verse does not point in only one direction. It turns the gaze outward toward the horizons, then inward toward the self. The same person is asked to study distance and intimacy, order and consciousness, the world that surrounds them and the world they carry.

---

> سنريهم اياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق أولم يكف بربك أنه على كل شيء شهيد
>
> *We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness?*

## The horizons

The horizons are more than a distant line. They include every widening field of knowledge: the scale of the heavens, the delicacy of living systems, the laws by which matter holds together, and the histories through which consequences become visible. Each discovery answers one question while opening another. For faith, that unfinished depth is not an embarrassment; it is part of the sign.

To look outward faithfully is not to force every scientific fact into a slogan. It is to receive knowledge with humility, refusing the illusion that explaining a process has exhausted its meaning.

## Within themselves

The inward signs are quieter. Memory, conscience, grief, love, intention, and the strange fact of awareness cannot be held at arm's length. The observer is included in the evidence. A person may hide from other people, but not from the moral knowledge that rises within them, nor from the One who knows what is concealed.

This makes the verse personal. It does not merely promise that signs will appear to humanity in general. It asks what has already become visible in your own life—and what response that visibility deserves.

## Witness

The final question changes the posture of the reader. God is not waiting for creation to inform Him. He is already Witness over all things. The signs are mercy for us: ways by which distracted sight becomes recognition, and recognition becomes responsibility.

So look again—not because belief is absent, but because belief is alive. Certainty is not always a single arrival. Sometimes it is the continual clearing of the lens.
