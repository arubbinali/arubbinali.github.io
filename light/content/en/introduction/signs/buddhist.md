*To the one formed by a Buddhist path*

# Look carefully at what appears.

Buddhist traditions train attention toward experience: change, attachment, suffering, compassion, and the instability of what we ordinarily call the self. The Quranic invitation begins with attention too, but directs that attention toward a different conclusion. It asks whether the world and the inward life are merely phenomena to observe, or signs through which their Creator makes the truth known.

> سنريهم اياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق أولم يكف بربك أنه على كل شيء شهيد
>
> *We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness?*

## Attention and meaning

Careful observation can reveal that everything encountered is dependent, changing, and unable to sustain itself. Islam agrees that created things do not possess permanence or independence in themselves. Yet it does not stop at impermanence. It asks what explains the existence of this dependent order at all.

The verse calls what is observed *signs*. A sign is not valuable only as an object of concentration; it points beyond itself. Its order, contingency, beauty, and intelligibility invite the question of intention.

## The horizons

The horizons include the vast world beyond immediate consciousness: living systems, cosmic order, causation, history, and the regularities that allow knowledge to exist. Each is changing, yet change itself occurs within a remarkably coherent reality.

The Quran does not identify this changing world with God. The Creator is neither one object inside the universe nor the sum of its processes. Allaah is the independent source upon whom every conditioned thing depends.

## Within themselves

Looking inward reveals thoughts, sensations, desires, and identities that arise and pass. It also reveals the observer's moral seriousness: the recognition that cruelty and compassion are not interchangeable, and that truth deserves something from us.

Islam understands the human being as more than an isolated, permanent ego, but it does not dissolve personal responsibility. A person is created, known, morally accountable, and capable of turning toward the One who created them.

## Compassion and the Creator

Compassion is not diminished by belief in a Creator; it is given an ultimate foundation. Every creature has dignity because it is created, and mercy becomes a response to the One whose mercy encompasses creation.

This reading asks you to bring disciplined attention with you. Look outward and inward without rushing, but also without deciding in advance that the signs cannot point beyond themselves. The Quran's claim is that sustained attention can become recognition: not only that suffering should end, but that creation has an Author, truth has a source, and every life returns to Him.
