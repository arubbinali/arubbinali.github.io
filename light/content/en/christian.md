*To the one who knows revelation*

# Begin with what is shared.

Before doctrines are compared, there is a familiar gesture: creation is read as meaningful. The heavens declare, the human heart responds, and the life of faith begins with the conviction that reality is more than an accident without address.

The Quran speaks here in the language of signs. It asks the reader to look outward and inward until truth becomes clear. A Christian can approach that claim without pretending differences do not exist, while still recognizing a shared grammar of revelation, witness, accountability, and wonder.

---

> سنريهم اياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق أولم يكف بربك أنه على كل شيء شهيد
>
> *We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness?*

## Creation as communication

In both Christian and Islamic thought, creation is not God, yet it points beyond itself. Its beauty does not prove every theological conclusion at once, but it unsettles the idea that matter is mute. Order, dependence, beauty, and intelligibility make the world readable.

The verse calls these realities *signs*, not possessions. A sign directs attention away from itself. The invitation is therefore not merely to admire the horizon, but to ask what—or whom—the horizon signifies.

## The inward witness

The movement inward may feel equally familiar. Conscience accuses and excuses. Love makes claims larger than appetite. Human beings hunger for forgiveness, permanence, and a goodness they repeatedly fail to embody. The self contains grandeur and fracture together.

This is not an argument that asks you to abandon careful judgment. It asks you to include the judge in the inquiry. What kind of creature searches for truth? Why does truth feel morally binding rather than merely useful?

## Reading across difference

Honest dialogue does not require flattening Jesus, scripture, prophecy, or salvation into interchangeable terms. Those differences matter. But understanding begins before agreement: by allowing another revelation to state its own claim and following the path of attention it proposes.

Read this verse as an invitation, not a demand for instant resolution. Look at the world, look within, and consider what it means for God to be sufficient as Witness. Shared light does not erase every boundary; it lets us see the boundary clearly enough to speak truthfully across it.
